About the event

Software that acts on other software.

A growing share of the web is driven by agents: bots that click, crawl, buy and fill in forms. Over one weekend, about 20 teams of four will build an agent, build a product that holds up against agents, or put an agent to work for a real user.

Computer Science leads the build

Architecture, automation, back end, front end. Turning the brief into a working product.

Cybersecurity owns half the score

Threat models, security checks, trap detection, safe data handling. Graded live.

The tracks

Pick your side of the agent.

Every track has one brief and five required outcomes, graded live by the judges. Teams mix CS and Cybersecurity students by design.

TRACK 01

Build the Agent

Testing Agent

Software ships faster than people can test it. Build an agent that you point at a live website. It explores the site on its own and reports real problems with evidence a developer can act on.

Brief
Website QA Bot

Takes a URL, returns a quality report: overall grade, score per area, plus a screenshot, steps to reproduce and a suggested fix for every finding.

Links & formsCheckout flowsAccessibilityPerformanceHeaders & cookiesExposed files
CSCrawler, browser automation, report interface
CYBERSecurity checks, severity ratings, fix guidance
Value: QA-team testing for small businesses at no cost per run.
Similar: Checkly, Mabl
TRACK 02

Survive the Agents

Agent-Proof Platform

A growing share of web traffic is automated. Build a real product for real users that keeps working correctly when automated clients try to abuse it.

Brief
Event Ticketing Platform

Ticketing for campus events: register & log in, browse events, buy tickets (max 4 per user per event), a QR ticket that admits once, and an organiser view of sales and check-ins.

Forged ticketsReused ticketsOver-limit buysOversellingOrder snoopingPrice tamperingPassword guessing
CSBack end, database, purchase flow, QR tickets, front end
CYBERThreat model, ticket signing, access control, rate limiting
Value: Fair access for fans, no revenue lost to scalper bots.
Similar: Platinumlist, DICE

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Who can take part?
The hackathon is designed for Computer Science and Cybersecurity students. Each team of four mixes both disciplines: CS leads the build, and Cybersecurity owns half of the score.
How big are teams, and how many will there be?
About 20 teams of four students. If you don't have a full team, register anyway and we'll help you find teammates from the other discipline.
Do I need paid AI credits or API keys?
No. An agent can be a browser automation bot (for example Playwright, Puppeteer or Selenium). Free, open-source tools are enough to score full marks.
Do we choose our track?
Track allocation will be confirmed before kickoff. Either teams choose their track or the organisers assign one to balance the field. All registered teams will be told in advance.
How is the winner decided?
50% comes from five required outcomes per track, each scored 0–3 live by the judges. The other 50% is split between Application of Technology (20%), Business Value (10%), Presentation (10%) and Originality (10%). Rubrics are published at kickoff.
What are the prizes?
AED 5,000 for 1st place, AED 3,000 for 2nd and AED 1,000 for 3rd. If the optional Live Showdown runs, it awards showcase prizes only and does not change track rankings.
Can we start building before the event?
Briefs and rubrics are published at kickoff, and all project code must be written during the hackathon. You're welcome to learn your tools beforehand: brush up on browser automation, auth and rate limiting.
What should I bring?
Your laptop, charger, student ID and anything that keeps you comfortable over two days. Food and Wi-Fi are on us.
